\title{Is the Reconstruction--Forecasting Tradeoff a Frontier\\or an Operating Point?}

\author{Anonymous Author(s)\\ Affiliation \\ Address \\ \texttt{email}}

\begin{abstract}
Autoencoder-based temporal models are routinely trained on a weighted sum of a
reconstruction loss and a forecasting loss, with the weight treated as a
hyperparameter. This practice assumes the balance is a genuine frontier. We ask
whether it is instead a suboptimal operating point induced by forcing one latent
to serve both objectives. On a controlled chaotic benchmark (Lorenz-63 lifted to
30 noisy channels, known intrinsic dimension 3) we split the representation into
an unbounded 3-d carrier read only by the forecaster and a sigmoid-bounded
$8\times8$ carrier read only by the decoder, trained in two stages so that the
forecast gradient provably cannot reach the decoder. At matched parameters,
matched wall clock and matched seeds, the split Pareto-dominates
\emph{every} setting of the weighted-loss knob that forecasts at all. We then
report the result that complicates this: the advantage is compute-dependent.
Given $10\times$ the wall clock, the balancing baseline overtakes the split on
both headline axes. What does not transfer with compute is stability --- bounded
decoding keeps out-of-range rollouts at $0\%$ against $100\%$ for every
unbounded baseline. We conclude the tradeoff is not one fixed frontier, and
argue that compute-matched comparison alone cannot settle whether it is
dissolvable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A large body of work trains one encoder to serve two objectives at once:
reconstruct the present and forecast the future. The standard mechanism is a
weighted loss, $\mathcal{L} = (1-\phi)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} +
\phi\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fcst}}$, with $\phi$ tuned per dataset. Coupled
Attention Networks \citep{coupledattn} state the weights explicitly as ``a
tradeoff between the prediction and reconstruction models''; CAAE
\citep{caae} names the underlying ``objective mismatch between training for
reconstruction fidelity and [the downstream task]''; a broad cluster of
reconstruction-plus-prediction hybrids follows the same recipe
\citep{memaae,hyvae}. In physics-informed sequence modelling, SSP
\citep{ssp} pursues the same separation by a different route --- spectral
truncation --- describing its contribution as ``decoupling reconstruction
fidelity from rollout regularity.''

Across these paradigms the tradeoff is treated as something to \emph{tune}.
Almost nobody asks whether the tuning is necessary. That is the question here.

\paragraph{What we are not claiming.}
Rate--distortion theory \citep{shannon,berger} proves a frontier between rate
and distortion for a given source, and the information bottleneck
\citep{tishby} extends this to predictive representations. We are not claiming
to beat any such bound. Rate--distortion constrains distortion \emph{at a fixed
rate}; it says nothing about which point inside the achievable region a given
architecture and schedule lands on. Our claim is only about the latter: at
fixed capacity and fixed compute, a shared latent under a weighted loss does
not reach the best available point, and separating the carriers moves it.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) We give a controlled, compute-matched test of whether the
reconstruction--forecasting tradeoff is a frontier or an operating point, on a
system whose true intrinsic dimension is known.
(ii) We show a bounded/unbounded latent split, trained in two stages,
Pareto-dominates every weighted-loss setting that forecasts at all, at matched
parameters, wall clock and seeds.
(iii) We report a negative result that constrains this: the dominance does not
survive a $10\times$ compute budget for the baseline.
(iv) We isolate which architectural ingredient buys what, using a bounded-shared-latent
control, and find boundedness and separation buy different things.

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Objects.}
An observation is $x_t \in \mathbb{R}^{n}$. Five learned maps plus a
forecaster:
\begin{equation*}
\begin{aligned}
E &: \mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{n}, & f &: \mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{k}, &
r &: \mathbb{R}^{n}\!\to\![0,1]^{a\times b},\\
m &: \mathbb{R}^{k}\!\to\![0,1]^{a\times b}, & g &: \mathbb{R}^{k}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{k}, &
D &: [0,1]^{a\times b}\!\to\!\mathbb{R}^{n}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation*}
Write $h_t = E(x_t)$, $b_t = f(h_t)$, $C_t = m(b_t)$. Here $b$ is the
\emph{unbounded} carrier ($k=3$) and $C$ the \emph{bounded} carrier
($a=b=8$). The reconstruction path is $x_t \to h_t \to b_t \to C_t \to
\hat{x}_t$; the forecast path steps only $b$ and recomputes $C$ from it,
$b_{t+1}=g(b_t)$, $C_{t+1}=m(b_{t+1})$, $\hat{x}_{t+1}=D(C_{t+1})$.

\paragraph{Consistency.}
The chain is only coherent if the indirect route to the bounded latent agrees
with the direct one, $m(f(h_t)) \approx r(h_t)$. Since $r$ sees all $n$
dimensions and $m$ sees only $k$, enforcing agreement forces $f$ to retain
everything $C$ needs while compressing $30\to3$. $r$ is a training-only
teacher and never runs at inference.

\paragraph{Two stages, and why the split is exact.}
Stage 1 trains $\theta_A=\{E,f,m,r,D\}$ on single timesteps:
$\mathcal{L}_A = \|D(m(f(E(x_t)))) - x_t\|^2 + \lambda\|m(f(E(x_t))) -
r(E(x_t))\|_F^2$. Stage 2 \emph{freezes} $\theta_A$, runs the frozen encoder
over every training timestamp to obtain the series $b_t$, and trains $g$ alone,
free-running, entirely in $b$-space:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_B(\theta_g) = \tfrac{1}{H}\sum_{\tau=1}^{H}
\big\|\hat{b}_{t+\tau} - b_{t+\tau}\big\|^2 ,
\qquad \hat{b}_{t+\tau} = g^{\circ\tau}(b_t).
\end{equation}
Neither $m$ nor $D$ appears in $\mathcal{L}_B$, so
$\partial\mathcal{L}_B/\partial\theta_A \equiv 0$: improving the forecast
\emph{cannot} degrade reconstruction. This is the decoupling, and it is an
identity rather than a penalty. Contrast the weighted loss, where
$\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fcst}}/\partial\theta_{\mathrm{enc}} \neq 0$ and
$\phi$ merely chooses which objective loses.

\paragraph{Boundedness gives unconditional stability.}
$m$ ends in a sigmoid, so $C_{t+\tau}\in[0,1]^{a\times b}$ by construction for
any input. $D$ is continuous on that compact set, hence
$\|\hat{x}_{t+\tau}\| \le \sup_{C\in[0,1]^{a\times b}}\|D(C)\| < \infty$ for
all $\tau$, \emph{independently of} $\hat{b}_{t+\tau}$. However far the
unbounded carrier drifts, the decoded trajectory cannot leave a bounded set:
error saturates instead of exploding. This costs zero parameters.

\section{Experimental setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
Lorenz-63 \citep{lorenz} at $dt=0.01$, lifted through a frozen random
$\tanh$ map to $n=30$ channels and corrupted with Gaussian noise at $5\%$ of
each channel's spread. True intrinsic dimension is 3, so $k$ is a probe rather
than a hyperparameter. Training pools 8 independent trajectories, each split
$70/15/15$ chronologically; 32 further trajectories are held out entirely,
never split and never seen in training. Every standardisation constant is fit
on the pooled training portion only. The reconstruction floor is
\emph{measured} from the realised noise draw in the final standardised scale
($0.002492$) rather than assumed as $\mathrm{noise}^2$.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
(B) the weighted-loss AE+GRU, $\phi$ swept over $\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.9,1\}$,
16-d shared latent; (D) the same construction with its \emph{shared} latent
sigmoid-bounded and kept bounded through rollout by a residual step in logit
space --- the control that holds boundedness fixed and removes the split;
(C) a physics-informed AE whose 3-d latent is calibrated to true Lorenz
coordinates and rolled out by RK4 on the known equations, with a misspecified
$\rho=26$ variant; plus persistence and climatology.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Every model gets \textbf{124{,}482 trainable parameters and 120\,s of wall
clock on one pinned CPU thread}. Ours, the PINN, $\phi\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5\}$ and
model D run 3 seeds; remaining $\phi$ settings run 1. We report reconstruction
MSE relative to the measured floor; valid prediction time (VPT, the lead time
at which normalised error first crosses $0.4$, in Lyapunov times
\citep{vlachas}); normalised error at 5 Lyapunov times; and \emph{divergence},
the fraction of rollouts leaving the training bounding box.

\paragraph{At matched compute, the split dominates.}
Table~\ref{tab:main} shows the split reaching $1.06\times$ the floor at VPT
$0.489$. Every $\phi$ setting that forecasts at all is worse on \emph{both}
axes simultaneously: the best of them reaches VPT $0.335$, and the only setting
with better reconstruction ($\phi{=}0$, $1.01\times$) has VPT $0.027$, below
persistence --- it does not forecast. So the split is not a different point on
the knob's curve; it is outside the region the knob reaches. The knob's own
curve is also nearly flat in VPT ($0.320$--$0.335$ across
$\phi\in[0.1,1]$) while reconstruction degrades by $3.8\times$, i.e. within
this family, paying reconstruction buys almost no forecast.

\paragraph{The negative result: this is compute-dependent.}
\label{sec:budget}
Held at 120\,s, the above reads as dissolution. It is not that simple. Given
$10\times$ the wall clock, $\phi{=}0.25$ reaches VPT $0.526$ at $0.99\times$
the floor --- better than the split on both headline axes. The shift
($\Delta$VPT $=0.205$) is an order of magnitude larger than the 3-seed spread
of the $1\times$ run ($\sigma=0.015$), so the weighted-loss family is
\emph{measurably undertrained} at the matched budget, not frontier-limited
there. We report this prominently because it bounds the claim: a
compute-matched Pareto comparison, the standard protocol in this literature,
can invert under a compute rescaling. Any claim of the form ``architecture $X$
dissolves the tradeoff,'' ours included, is only as strong as the budget it was
measured at.

\paragraph{What does not follow compute: stability.}
Divergence separates the models in a way compute does not fix. Every unbounded
baseline leaves the training range on $100\%$ of rollouts at $1\times$; at
$10\times$ that only falls to $22\%$. The split is at $0\%$, and its
error at 5 Lyapunov times is $1.18$ --- at the climatology level $1.00$, i.e.
saturating rather than exploding, exactly as the boundedness argument predicts.
This is a guarantee from the function class, not a quantity that better
optimisation can supply.

\paragraph{Which ingredient buys what.}
Model D holds boundedness fixed and removes the split. It reaches divergence
$1.6\%$ --- essentially the split's stability --- but reconstruction
$2.28\times$ floor and VPT $0.329$, no better than the knob. So the two
ingredients buy different things: \textbf{boundedness buys stability;
separating the carriers buys the reconstruction and forecast quality.} Neither
alone reproduces the result, which is the cleanest evidence we have that the
split is doing real work rather than acting as a reparameterised $\phi$.

\paragraph{Two-stage schedule.}
Training the identical modules end-to-end with the forecast objective included
degrades reconstruction $4.4\times$ ($0.0026\to0.0117$) and VPT to $0.372$.
The architecture alone is not sufficient; the schedule that makes
$\partial\mathcal{L}_B/\partial\theta_A=0$ true is load-bearing.

\paragraph{Noise.}
Re-running the headline comparison at observation noise $0.15$ and $0.30$
(1 seed), the split leads the best $\phi$ at every level --- gaps
$+0.163$, $+0.290$, $+0.208$ in VPT --- but the gap is \emph{not monotonic},
peaking at $0.15$. We report the sequence rather than a direction. Divergence
for the split stays at $0\%$ throughout.

\paragraph{Physics baseline.}
The PINN needed a fix to be a fair comparator: its decoder reads the raw latent,
never the physically-calibrated coordinates, so nothing constrained that
latent's scale and a purely \emph{relative} physics residual was satisfiable in
a self-consistent but physically wrong coordinate system. Recalibrating the
latent-to-state affine by least squares each epoch moved its forecast loss from
$17.6$ (rising during training) to $0.015$ and its state-recovery $R^2$ from
$[0.64,0.70,0.10]$ to $[0.996,0.989,0.981]$. Post-fix it is the strongest
baseline on VPT ($0.378$) and has the best 50-Lyapunov-time attractor fidelity
in the comparison (Wasserstein $0.068$ vs the split's $0.170$).

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{One system.} All results are Lorenz-63 with one lift; the cross-domain
phenomenon claim motivating this work is not tested here.
\textbf{Compute-dependence is unresolved.} We did not run $10\times$ with 3
seeds, locate the crossover, or give the split $10\times$.
\textbf{Fixed capacity.} The bounded carrier is a fixed $8\times8$ grid; the
adaptive-precision mechanism motivating the broader hypothesis is untested.
\textbf{Uneven seeds.} Single-seed rows report $\sigma=0$ because $n=1$, not
because they are stable.
\textbf{A floor artifact.} $\phi{=}0.25$ at $10\times$ sits at $0.99\times$ the
measured floor; a capacity-matched control ($\mathrm{latent}{=}3$) moves cleanly
above it, indicating a wide unregularised latent exploiting cross-channel
redundancy rather than a leak.
\textbf{Constructed baselines.} The $\phi$ sweep and model D are the balancing
recipe in its plainest form, not named published methods.

\section{Conclusion}

At matched parameters, wall clock and seeds, separating a bounded
decoder-facing carrier from an unbounded forecaster-facing carrier reaches an
operating point no setting of the weighted-loss knob reaches, and the knob's own
curve is nearly flat --- within that family, sacrificing reconstruction buys
almost no forecast. That is evidence the tradeoff, as practised, is an
operating point rather than a frontier. But the advantage is compute-dependent,
and we show it inverting at $10\times$ budget. The property that does not
depend on compute is stability: bounded decoding gives $0\%$ divergence by
construction, where every unbounded baseline diverges on every rollout. We
therefore read the honest conclusion as narrower than ``the tradeoff
dissolves'': it is not a single fixed frontier, compute-matched comparison
alone cannot settle whether it is dissolvable, and the reliability half of the
problem is separable from the accuracy half.

\section{Reproducibility}

Code, data generator and the executed notebook (all outputs stored) accompany
this submission. All numbers come from a single end-to-end run at
\texttt{QUICK=False} on one pinned CPU thread; the full run takes
approximately three hours from an empty checkpoint directory. Every model is
cached after training, so re-running reproduces the tables without retraining.
Architecture: $E$ is an MLP $30\!\to\!128\!\to\!128\!\to\!30$; $f$ is
$30\!\to\!128\!\to\!64\!\to\!3$; $m$ is $3\!\to\!64\!\to\!128\!\to\!64$ with a
sigmoid; $r$ is $30\!\to\!128\!\to\!128\!\to\!64$ with a sigmoid; $D$ is
$64\!\to\!128\!\to\!128\!\to\!30$; $g$ is a residual GRU
$(3\!\to\!64)$ with a linear head. All activations GELU. Adam, $\eta=10^{-3}$,
$\lambda=1$, stage-1 batch 256, stage-2 batch 64, warm-up 64 steps, training
rollout 20 steps, evaluation horizon 550 steps ($5$ Lyapunov times).
