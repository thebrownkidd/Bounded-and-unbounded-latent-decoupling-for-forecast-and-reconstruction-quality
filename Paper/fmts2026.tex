\title{Is the Reconstruction--Forecasting Tradeoff a Frontier\\or an Operating Point?}

\author{Arpit Goel\\ TwinSim Labs \\ arpit@thebrownkid.in}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Autoencoder-based temporal models are routinely trained on a weighted sum of a
reconstruction loss and a forecasting loss, with the weight treated as a
hyperparameter. This practice assumes the balance is a genuine frontier. We ask
whether it is instead a suboptimal operating point induced by forcing one latent
to serve both objectives. On a controlled chaotic benchmark (Lorenz-63 lifted to
30 noisy channels, known intrinsic dimension 3) we split the representation into
an unbounded 3-d carrier read only by the forecaster and a sigmoid-bounded
$8\times8$ carrier read only by the decoder, trained in two stages so that the
forecast gradient provably cannot reach the decoder. At matched parameters,
matched wall clock and matched seeds, the split Pareto-dominates \emph{every} setting of the
weighted-loss knob that forecasts at all. We then report the result that
complicates this: the advantage is compute-dependent --- given $10\times$ the
wall clock, the balancing baseline overtakes it on both headline axes. What does
not transfer with compute is stability: bounded decoding holds out-of-range
rollouts at $0\%$ against $100\%$ for every unbounded baseline. The tradeoff is
therefore not one fixed frontier, and compute-matched comparison alone cannot
settle whether it is dissolvable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

A large body of work trains one encoder to serve two objectives at once:
reconstruct the present and forecast the future. The standard mechanism is a
weighted loss, $\mathcal{L} = (1-\phi)\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rec}} +
\phi\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fcst}}$, with $\phi$ tuned per dataset. Coupled
Attention Networks \citep{coupledattn} call their weights ``a tradeoff between
the prediction and reconstruction models''; CAAE \citep{caae} names the
``objective mismatch between training for reconstruction fidelity and testing
for discriminative decisions''; reconstruction-plus-prediction hybrids fuse
both objectives onto one shared latent, some with explicit weights
\citep{memaae}, some by plainly summing the two losses \citep{hyvae}; and SSP
\citep{ssp} pursues the same separation by spectral truncation, ``decoupling
reconstruction fidelity from rollout regularity.'' Across all of them the
tradeoff is something to \emph{tune}. Almost nobody asks whether the tuning is
necessary. The ingredients exist separately --- bounded latents are routine in
representation learning \citep{fsq}, and Koopman-style forecasters constrain
the latent \emph{dynamics} to buy long-horizon stability \citep{azencot,koopa}
--- but neither line splits the two objectives across separate carriers, and
the Koopman forecasters still run a single latent through both.

\paragraph{What we are not claiming.}
Rate--distortion theory \citep{shannon,berger} proves a frontier between rate
and distortion for a source, and the information bottleneck \citep{tishby}
extends this to predictive representations. We are not claiming to beat any such
bound. Rate--distortion constrains distortion \emph{at a fixed rate}; it says
nothing about which point inside the achievable region a given architecture and
schedule lands on. Our claim is only the latter: at fixed capacity and compute,
a shared latent under a weighted loss does not reach the best available point,
and separating the carriers moves it.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
(i) A controlled, compute-matched test of whether the tradeoff is a frontier or
an operating point, on a system of known intrinsic dimension. (ii) A
bounded/unbounded split Pareto-dominating every weighted-loss setting that
forecasts at all, at matched parameters, wall clock and seeds. (iii) A negative
result constraining this: the dominance does not survive a $10\times$ baseline
budget. (iv) A bounded-shared-latent control isolating which ingredient buys
what.

\section{Method}

\paragraph{Objects.}
For $x_t\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$ write 
$h_t=E(x_t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}$,

$b_t=f(h_t)\in\mathbb{R}^{k}$ and $C_t=m(b_t)\in[0,1]^{a\times b}$
with a decoder 
$D:[0,1]^{a\times b}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}$
a forecaster

$g:\mathbb{R}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}^{k}$
and a teacher

$r:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,1]^{a\times b}$
Here $b$ is the \emph{unbounded}
carrier ($k=3$) and $C$ the \emph{bounded} one ($a=b=8$). Reconstruction runs
$x_t\to h_t\to b_t\to C_t\to\hat{x}_t$; forecasting steps only $b$ and
recomputes $C$ from it: 
$b_{t+1}=g(b_t)$, $C_{t+1}=m(b_{t+1})$,
$\hat{x}_{t+1}=D(C_{t+1})$.

\paragraph{Consistency.}
The chain is coherent only if the indirect route to the bounded latent agrees
with the direct one, $m(f(h_t)) \approx r(h_t)$. As $r$ sees all $n$ dimensions
and $m$ only $k$, agreement forces $f$ to retain everything $C$ needs while
compressing $30\to3$. $r$ is a training-only teacher, never run at inference.

\paragraph{Two stages, and why the split is exact.}
Stage 1 trains 
$\theta_A=\{E,f,m,r,D\}$ on single timesteps:

$\mathcal{L}_A = \|D(m(f(E(x_t)))) - x_t\|^2 + \lambda\|m(f(E(x_t))) -
r(E(x_t))\|_F^2$
Stage 2 \emph{freezes} $\theta_A$, runs the frozen encoder over every training timestamp to obtain the series $b_t$, and trains $g$ alone,
free-running, entirely in $b$-space:

$\mathcal{L}_B(\theta_g) = \tfrac{1}{H}\sum_{\tau=1}^{H}\|\hat{b}_{t+\tau} -
b_{t+\tau}\|^2$ with $\hat{b}_{t+\tau} = g^{\circ\tau}(b_t)$

Neither $m$ nor $D$ appears in $\mathcal{L}_B$, so

$\partial\mathcal{L}_B/\partial\theta_A \equiv 0$: improving the forecast
\emph{cannot} degrade reconstruction. This is the decoupling, and it is an
identity rather than a penalty. Contrast the weighted loss, where

$\partial\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{fcst}}/\partial\theta_{\mathrm{enc}} \neq 0$ and
$\phi$ 
merely chooses which objective loses.

\paragraph{Boundedness gives unconditional stability.}
$m$ ends in a sigmoid, so $C_{t+\tau}\in[0,1]^{a\times b}$ by construction. $D$
is continuous on that compact set, hence $\|\hat{x}_{t+\tau}\| \le
\sup_{C}\|D(C)\| < \infty$ for all $\tau$, \emph{independently of}
$\hat{b}_{t+\tau}$: however far the unbounded carrier drifts, the decoded
trajectory cannot leave a bounded set, so error saturates instead of exploding.
This costs zero parameters.

\section{Experimental setup}

\paragraph{Data.}
Lorenz-63 \citep{lorenz} at $dt=0.01$, lifted through a frozen random $\tanh$
map to $n=30$ channels with Gaussian noise at $5\%$ of each channel's spread.
True intrinsic dimension is 3, so $k$ is a probe, not a hyperparameter. Training
pools 8 independent trajectories, each split $70/15/15$ chronologically; 32
further trajectories are held out entirely. Every standardisation constant is
fit on the pooled training portion only, and the reconstruction floor is
\emph{measured} from the realised noise draw ($0.002492$) rather than assumed
as $\mathrm{noise}^2$.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
(B) weighted-loss AE+GRU, $\phi\in\{0,0.1,0.25,0.5,0.75,0.9,1\}$, 16-d shared
latent; (D) the same with its \emph{shared} latent sigmoid-bounded through
rollout --- the control holding boundedness fixed while removing the split;
(C) a physics-informed AE whose 3-d latent is calibrated to true Lorenz
coordinates and rolled out by RK4 on the known equations, plus a misspecified
$\rho=26$ variant; and persistence/climatology.

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Every model gets \textbf{124{,}482 trainable parameters and 120\,s of wall clock
on one pinned CPU thread}. Ours, the PINN, $\phi\in\{0.1,0.25,0.5\}$ and model D
run 3 seeds; remaining $\phi$ settings run 1. We report reconstruction MSE over
the measured floor; VPT (lead time at which normalised error first crosses
$0.4$, in Lyapunov times \citep{vlachas}); error at 5 Lyapunov times; and
\emph{divergence}, the fraction of rollouts leaving the training box.

\paragraph{At matched compute, the split dominates.}
The split reaches $1.06\times$ the floor at VPT $0.489$. Every $\phi$ that
forecasts at all is worse on \emph{both} axes at once: the best reaches $0.335$
($\phi{=}0.9$, App.~A), and the only one reconstructing better ($\phi{=}0$,
$1.01\times$) has VPT $0.027$, below persistence --- it does not forecast. The
split is thus not another point on the knob's curve but outside the region it
reaches. That curve is itself nearly flat ($0.320$--$0.335$ across
$\phi\in[0.1,1]$) while reconstruction degrades $3.8\times$: paying
reconstruction buys almost no forecast here.

\paragraph{The negative result: this is compute-dependent.}
\label{sec:budget}
Held at 120\,s the above reads as dissolution; it is not that simple. Given
$10\times$ the wall clock, $\phi{=}0.25$ reaches VPT $0.526$ at $0.99\times$ the
floor --- better than the split on both headline axes. The shift ($\Delta$VPT
$=0.205$) is an order of magnitude larger than the $1\times$ run's 3-seed spread
($\sigma=0.015$), so the family is \emph{measurably undertrained} at that
budget, not frontier-limited there. This bounds the claim: a compute-matched
Pareto comparison, the standard protocol here, can invert under a compute
rescaling. Any claim that architecture $X$ dissolves the tradeoff, ours
included, is only as strong as the budget it was measured at.

\paragraph{What does not follow compute: stability.}
Divergence separates the models in a way compute does not fix. Every unbounded
baseline leaves the training range on $100\%$ of rollouts at $1\times$; at
$10\times$ that falls only to $22\%$. The split is at $0\%$, with error at 5
Lyapunov times of $1.18$ --- the climatology level, i.e. saturating rather than
exploding, as the boundedness argument predicts. That is a guarantee from the
function class, not something better optimisation supplies.

\paragraph{Which ingredient buys what.}
Model D holds boundedness fixed and removes the split. It reaches divergence
$1.6\%$ --- essentially the split's stability --- but reconstruction
$2.28\times$ floor and VPT $0.329$, no better than the knob. The two ingredients
buy different things: \textbf{boundedness buys stability; separating the
carriers buys the reconstruction and forecast quality.} Neither alone reproduces
the result --- our cleanest evidence that the split does real work rather than
acting as a reparameterised $\phi$.

\paragraph{Schedule, noise, physics.}
End-to-end training of the same modules degrades reconstruction $4.4\times$ and
VPT to $0.372$: the schedule, not just the architecture, is load-bearing. At
noise $0.15$/$0.30$ (1 seed) the split still leads the best $\phi$ --- VPT gaps
$+0.163$, $+0.290$, $+0.208$ --- but \emph{not monotonically}, peaking at
$0.15$; we report the sequence, not a direction. The PINN needed a repair to be
a fair comparator: its decoder reads the uncalibrated latent, so a
\emph{relative} physics residual was satisfiable in physically wrong
coordinates. Recalibrating each epoch moved its forecast loss $17.6\to0.015$;
post-fix it has the best 50-Lyapunov-time attractor fidelity here (Wasserstein
$0.068$ vs the split's $0.170$). Appendices~B--D.

\section{Limitations}

\textbf{One system:} Lorenz-63, one lift; the cross-domain phenomenon claim
motivating this work is untested here. \textbf{Compute-dependence unresolved:}
we did not run $10\times$ with 3 seeds, locate the crossover, or give the split
$10\times$. \textbf{Fixed capacity:} the bounded carrier is a fixed $8\times8$
grid and we never varied it, so how much of the result rests on that
particular size is unknown. \textbf{Uneven seeds:} single-seed rows report $\sigma=0$ because
$n=1$. \textbf{A floor artifact:} $\phi{=}0.25$ at $10\times$ sits at
$0.99\times$ the measured floor; a capacity-matched control
($\mathrm{latent}{=}3$) clears it, indicating a wide unregularised latent
exploiting cross-channel redundancy rather than a leak. \textbf{Constructed
baselines:} the $\phi$ sweep and model D are the balancing recipe in its
plainest form, not named published methods.

\section{Conclusion}

At matched parameters, wall clock and seeds, separating a bounded
decoder-facing carrier from an unbounded forecaster-facing one reaches an
operating point no setting of the knob reaches, on a curve that is itself nearly
flat. But that advantage is compute-dependent: we show it inverting at
$10\times$ budget. What does not depend on compute is stability --- bounded
decoding gives $0\%$ divergence by construction where every unbounded baseline
diverges on every rollout. The honest conclusion is narrower than ``the tradeoff
dissolves'': it is not a single fixed frontier, compute-matched comparison alone
cannot settle whether it is dissolvable, and the reliability half of the problem
is separable from the accuracy half.
